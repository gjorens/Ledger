File location
Where your ledger file is kept

Choose the folder where your ledger lives: iCloud Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive or any other folder on this computer. The app saves ledger.json inside it and autosaves there. To make a new folder, type a name below first; it is created inside the folder you pick.

Browsers only tell the app the name of the folder you chose, not its full path.

Categories

Add a category
Your categories

Renaming updates every entry and recurring item that uses the category. Renaming one to the name of another merges them. Deleting leaves the affected entries uncategorised.

Archive old data

Over the years the ledger file grows. Archiving moves old reconciled entries (and deleted ones) into a separate file, ledger-archive-YYYY-MM.json, and keeps balances exactly as they are. Nothing is lost: open the archive any time with View an archive…. Entries from the latest reconciled month, and anything not yet reconciled, always stay in your ledger.

About myLedger
myLedger

A personal bank-transaction ledger that runs entirely on your device. Your data is only ever stored in the file you choose and in a safety copy inside this browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

User guide

Getting started

  • First time: tap Create first account, or Open file to load a ledger you already have.
  • Add more accounts with + Add account (chequing, savings, credit card, loan, and so on). Edit account changes its details, opening balance and low-balance warning.
  • Switch accounts with the tabs. Each account has its own balance, recurring items and reconciliation.

Adding items (+ New item)

  • Type: a payment or deposit, or a transfer between two of your accounts (it creates matching entries in both).
  • Repeats: choose weekly, every 2 weeks, monthly, every 3 months or yearly to make a recurring item. The amount is then an estimate.
  • Amounts: type digits only; the decimal point is fixed (1 2 5 0 gives 12.50). The ± button, or typing a minus sign, switches between money in and money out. On a credit card or loan, + means a charge and − means a payment.
  • Start typing a description to pick an earlier one; its category and amount are filled in.
  • Add another keeps the window open and the date unchanged, which is handy for entering a statement.

The transaction list

  • Sorted by date, then credits before debits, then description. The list opens at your latest past transaction.
  • Edit changes any entry. Delete marks it deleted: it stays in the file but no longer counts.
  • Italic rows are projected from recurring items. est. means estimated. Edit a projected row to enter the amount the biller confirmed; it then shows in bold.
  • Each account can have a currency (CAD, USD, EUR, GBP, MXN and others) in Edit account. Balances then show with the right symbol. There is no conversion between currencies, so a transfer between accounts in different currencies asks you to confirm, and reports across mixed-currency accounts carry a warning.
  • Transfers are described automatically as From account \u2192 To account and the description cannot be edited (it follows account renames). Amounts are positive only: use the double-arrow button between the two account boxes to swap the direction.
  • Archive old data (Settings) moves old reconciled entries into a separate file, ledger-archive-YYYY-MM.json, so your ledger stays small. Balances do not change. Open an archive any time with View an archive (read-only, searchable). Keep the archive files with your ledger: they are the only copy of those entries.
  • Row buttons are icons (hover for the name): pencil = Edit, trash = Delete, check = Post, skip arrow = Skip, back arrow = Unpost, square = Stop. A deleted entry stays in the list marked deleted with a round arrow button to undelete it (a transfer comes back on both sides).
  • Post turns a projected row into a real entry; Skip drops that one occurrence. If you post by mistake, press Unpost on the entry to put it back.
  • In Recurring items: Change amount schedules a new estimate from a future date, and Stop ends the item.
  • Look ahead sets how far projections run. Balance graph compares the 12 months ending at that date with the same 12 months a year earlier.

Monthly reconciliation

  • Open Monthly reconciliation, pick the statement month, enter the closing balance from your bank statement, and tick every item that appears on it.
  • When the difference is zero, press Mark month reconciled. Use Edit on unticked items, or Add missed item for something on the statement that is not in your ledger.
  • After the first reconciled month, balances count from the latest statement balance; the opening balance is only the starting point until then.

Credit cards and loans

  • These show the amount owing as a positive number, charges as positive and payments as negative. Behind the scenes the numbers are stored the same way as other accounts.

Low-balance warning

  • Set a level in Edit account. If a change makes a future balance drop below it, a popup appears and the account tab gets a red ring for as long as that stays true. For a card, the level is an amount owing that you do not want to exceed.

Reports

  • Open Reports to see spending and income by category (or by category and month) for a period you choose, for one account or all of them. Transfers, deleted items and projections are left out.

Saving

  • Chrome, Edge or Brave on a Mac: choose a folder in Settings. The app then autosaves to it every 30 seconds and when you leave the page. If it asks you to Reconnect after a restart, tap it once.
  • Safari, iPhone and iPad: press Save file (or Save to Files) after making changes, choose the folder, and choose Replace. Press Open file to load your ledger on another device.
  • An unsaved-changes banner shows when there is anything not yet saved. A safety copy is also kept inside the browser.
  • Use the same version of the app on all your devices, and open the latest file before you start entering items.

Test data

  • To practise without touching your real ledger, open ledger-test.json. The file is marked as test data inside itself, so the app shows an orange stripe and a TEST DATA tag whenever it is open, on any device. (A file named like test data but not marked asks you to confirm once, then is marked.) It is saved under the same name.
